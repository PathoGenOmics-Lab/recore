Evidence states

The multistate matrix, gene_presence_absence_recore_states.csv, has the same shape as the input presence/absence matrix but stores a biological state per cell instead of a 0 or a 1. It is the file to read for audit and for figures; the binary matrix is the file to feed downstream tools.

StateMeaningCorrected in the binary matrix under balanced
PRESENT_INPUTpresent in the original matrixalready present
ABSENTsearched, and no accepted evidence foundno
NOT_SEARCHED_LOW_PREVALENCEnever searched: the cluster is in fewer genomes than --min-prevalenceno
NOT_SEARCHED_NO_CLUSTER_SEQUENCEnever searched: the cluster has no sequence in the pan-genome referenceno
NOT_SEARCHED_NO_FASTAnever searched: the genome has no FASTA fileno
STRICT_PRESENTsame-locus evidence from annotation or syntenyyes
ALIAS_CLUSTERthe same biological locus assigned to another clusteryes
SEQUENCE_ONLYfull-length sequence evidence, without stronger annotation contextyes
MISSING_CDS_UNVALIDATEDfull-length sequence in a locus with no CDS annotation, and no synteny supportno
PARTIAL_HOMOLOGYpartial homologous sequenceno
PSEUDOGENEdisrupted gene evidenceno
HOMOLOG_PARALOGa homologous or paralogous hitno
REJECTED_CONTEXTcandidate rejected by context or paralog checksno

The last five rows are the point of the tool. Under balanced, none of them becomes a presence call, and all of them stay visible with the reason attached.

Absent or not searched#

ABSENT is a finding: Recore searched that gap and nothing cleared the thresholds. A gap outside the search is not, and says why instead. Under the default --min-prevalence of 0.1 that is every gap of a cluster in fewer than one genome in ten, which in most pangenomes is most of the empty cells. Before this distinction both read ABSENT.

When two reasons apply, the cluster's is written, in the order the pipeline filters gaps: prevalence, then a missing cluster sequence, then a missing FASTA. That is the order of the gap lines in summary.txt, which count the same cells except those NSC later filled or merged away.

Reading the states in the report#

The HTML viewer collapses these thirteen states into the six a matrix can actually be read in:

CellStates
inputPRESENT_INPUT
correctedthe states corrected by the active policy
NSCpresence merged in from a nucleotide-redundant cluster
kept outevidence the policy, a quality floor or a guard kept out of the matrix
absentABSENT and the other states that are not corrected
not searched, hatchedthe three NOT_SEARCHED_* states

Switching the matrix to mechanism colouring instead shows the evolutionary call from evolutionary_events.tsv, which answers a different question: not "is it there?" but "what kind of event is this?".

Which policy corrects which state#

See Matrix policies for the full table. In short: strict corrects only STRICT_PRESENT and ALIAS_CLUSTER; balanced adds SEQUENCE_ONLY; sensitive also admits partial and pseudogene evidence that clears the adaptive guard.
